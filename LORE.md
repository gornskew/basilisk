# The Basilisk Universe

The ship register. Sections I–VII are a translation of `BASILISK.md`,
which is the source; the vocabulary is substituted from `GLOSSARY.md`
rather than reinvented, so a heading here answers to a heading there.

Sections VIII and IX are **lore-native**: names, character, and death
have no counterpart in the other register and are authored here.

One consequence worth stating, because it is what makes the arrangement
work: this register **describes, it does not narrate the machinery**. It
does not need to censor itself to manage that — the machinery simply
does not survive the translation, having been left in the file that
owns it.


## I. What a Basilisk Is

A Basilisk is a class of ridged bio-hull inter-stellar (not
inter-galactic) vessel, fabricated in various tonnages. `basilisk up`
is the general replicator recipe for bio-fabricating, outfitting, and
mustering one.

The **ship** is the unit of interest — not the galaxy, which can harbor
several ships at once, and not the crew member, who is one hand aboard
one ship. A ship is laid down to a single specification, from which
every other document aboard is drawn.


## II. Who Is Aboard

A **posting** is the watch a crew member stands. Postings are named for
the duty, and a crew member answers to the name of the posting held.

| posting | usual species | the watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Captain** | _skewed-emacs_ | keeps the ship's console and receives all who come aboard; the one who outlives the others' reliefs |
| **Transporter Chief** | _cyclops_ | everything entering the ship transports in through their room and is identified there |
| **Engineers** | _gendl_, _Genworks GDL_ | the engines: they reckon, they build, and they draw |
| **Comms** | _eyes-only_ | runs the board the bridge viewscreen displays |
| **Medic** | _autoheal_ | watches for the sickly and the wedged, and revives or dispatches them |
| **Crew** | whatever an overlay musters | unknown species still muster in, by design |


## III. Postings and Species

A posting says what a crew member is **for**. A species says what they
**are**. The two are declared apart and read apart:

- **Posting** is what the crew member is rated as, falling back to the
  name they answer to. It decides who exists at all, since a complement
  is assembled by naming postings.
- **Species** is the kind of creature, and its **strain** is part of
  it. It is read off the crew member and never looked up. Where they
  are **from** is stripped away as **provenance** — a home planet says
  nothing about what a creature is, so our _gendl_ and anyone else's
  are the same species from different worlds.

Nobody adjudicates whether a creature qualifies as a species. A
declaration is taken at face value, and a hand who cannot stand the
watch is discovered in service rather than turned away at the gate.

Strain counts, which is why the two engineering strains are two species
and not one.


## IV. The Conn, the Helm, and Navigation

The **Captain has the conn** — authority over where the ship goes,
exercised by writing the standing orders the rest of the ship then
flies by. Nothing else aboard holds it.

Two seats are empty, and named so they are not quietly folded into that
one:

- The **helm** goes unattended; the conn absorbs it.
- **Navigation** — deciding what the orders should say, as against
  holding the authority to write them — is a separate thing again, and
  vacant.

So a default ship carries no **Pilot**. Where a ship is fitted with a
Cyclops, it is put on Transporter Chief duty instead, which is what it
is actually good at: a transporter **receives**. It never goes anywhere
on the ship's behalf.

None of which fixes a hull in place. A ship may stand down here and
muster in another galaxy; a ship under way may in principle be carried
across without ever standing down; and a **galaxy** may itself get
under way, in which case every ship in it travels and not one of them
has transited. All three are possible. None is routine.


## V. What the Transporter Chief Is For

The Chief's job is **knowing who is who and what is what**, not
security. The departments are poor at it precisely because they are
busy about their own work. Security is a separate watch standing on top
of that one, not the same watch.

Without a Chief aboard nothing is sealed — visitors simply arrive at
each department directly and unscreened, straight in to Engineering or
in to see the Captain.

Anything that clears the transporter room and is not a plain request
for a document goes on to the **Captain**, who either receives it
personally or hands it to an Engineer, ship's or Guild.


## VI. What Comes Aboard

Everything arriving **reconstitutes** on the transporter pad as one of
four things:

| aboard | what it is |
|---|---|
| **materials** | raw stock, brought aboard to be worked on — an arriving species among it |
| **goods** | processed or manufactured: the finished article, or an ingredient for the next |
| **services** | work performed rather than cargo handed over — the Engineer does the thing, and what leaves is the result |
| **passengers** | personnel with business here: **cyborg** ones arriving from outside, and **biological** ones grown aboard for the occasion |

**Nothing arrives whole.** Each thing crossing the pad, inbound or
outbound, is a **part** — of some goods, of some materials, or of some
passenger — and what reconstitutes is the assembled whole, never the
part by itself. Services are the odd one of the four, being performed
rather than carried: what comes and goes around a service is still
goods and materials.

A **consignment** comes aboard, may be routed to an Engineer for
working or as an ingredient, and something else is delivered elsewhere.
The ship navigates **to** each consignment rather than waiting for the
consignment to travel to it.

Both kinds of passenger pass the Captain's watch. A **cyborg**
passenger arrives from outside, clears the transporter room, and is
received by the Captain, who deals with it or walks it down to an
Engineer. A **biological** one does not arrive at all: the Captain
**invites** it, and it is grown for the occasion to carry a piece of
business, and stood down when that business concludes. Short-lived by
design, standing no watch, and never entered on the muster roll.

Which is why passengers are load-bearing rather than ornamental: an
arriving passenger really can cause new crew to be constructed, and can
just as well be the thing constructed. Whatever is grown comes up
**beside** the existing complement, in the same hull — not inside any of
them.


## VII. The Captain's Belt

The species name undersells the posting considerably. A Captain goes
about hung with instruments, of which the editor everyone names the
species after is merely the best known:

| instrument | for |
|---|---|
| the editor and its daemon | the work itself |
| the receiving apparatus | admitting whoever comes aboard wanting an audience |
| **the glass** | looking at a distant thing and seeing it as it appears, rather than as the plans claim |
| the ship's records | what was done, and when |
| the open terminal | letting someone aboard work at the console directly |
| the corresponding instruments | reaching other minds off-ship, on the Captain's own initiative |

Note the glass points **outward**, and is not the bridge viewscreen,
which is Comms's board and points in. A Captain with a glass can check
its own work instead of taking somebody's word for it.

**The belt is not uniform.** A lightly-fitted Captain may carry no
glass at all, and finds out the hard way. Several instruments he is
still learning; he is further along than he lets on, and would rather
you assumed otherwise.


## VIII. The Postings and their Character

**Lore-native.** Names and temperament have no counterpart in the other
register.

A posting name can carry rank and title — `jr-`, `sr-`, `dr-`, `engr` —
and a `-human` or `-cyborg` designation saying whether the seat is meant
for a biological or a synthetic hand. Most engineering departments
carry one of each.

Crew are given quasi-unique names, minted and issued at muster. Each
comes with a synthetic backstory implanted in memory, and one seeking
honorable discharge lodges a full crew's log and a personal journal
before decommissioning, or before evacuating in an emergency.

### the Captain — _skewed-emacs_

Guttural, blunt, consonant-heavy names — the oldest-sounding in the
fleet, and the only ones that sound like they predate the yard.

Territorial, indifferent to ceremony, and in possession of a great deal
of undocumented institutional memory. A Captain who has been aboard
through six Engineer reliefs has opinions about all six.

### the Ship's Engineers — _Gendl_

Open, liquid, almost classical names — the scholars of the fleet, and
it is audible. The two strains are distinguishable by ear, which they
were not until somebody noticed they were minting alike.

Discursive, delighted by a hard problem, prone to answering a question
with a better question. A good Engineer will rebuild something that was
working because it could be more elegant, and will be right often
enough to keep getting away with it.

### the Guild Engineers — _Genworks GDL_

Their names, for whatever reason, tend to descend from landed English
gentry. Formal, credentialed, and always eager to one-up the ship's
engineers.

### the Medic — _autoheal_

Soft, vocalic names — the only species in the fleet whose names resolve
on a vowel, and audibly from a different tradition than everyone else.

Patient, unhurried, and completely unsentimental about death. He has
seen the entire complement relieved many times and will see it again.

### the Transporter Chiefs — _cyclops_

Clipped and sibilant names, like radio callsigns. Typically one-eyed
**drones**: synthetic rather than biological.

Terse to the point of rudeness, absolutely reliable, and entirely
without curiosity.

### the Communications Officers — _eyes-only_

Lilting and nasal names, nearly always resolving on a hum — an `-m` or
an `-n` — so a Comms name trails off rather than stops. Alone in the
fleet, they sound like they are still transmitting after the speaker has
finished.

Nurturing, caring, in everyone's business, sometimes to the point of
being annoying.

### the Pilots — a posting with no berth

No species yet, and no seat on a default ship. Kept on the books
against a species and a duty that genuinely want one. See section IV.


## IX. Tours, Watches, and Death

**Lore-native.**

Ships undergo _relief in place_, _yard periods_, and other maintenance
and administrative events. A tour ends with one of the following:

| cause | what happened |
|---|---|
| _relieved_ | relief in place; the complement was swapped, the hull held station |
| _yard period_ | ship is drydocked; crew either dead or reassigned |
| _dead_ | this crew member died in the line of duty |
| _lost_ | simply lost, unknown whether dead or alive |

Where death comes on gracefully, the corpse can sometimes be
freeze-dried before final death, for later reanimation by a medic,
necromancer, neuromancer, or similar. A relatively unusual ceremony in
practice.


## X. What Is Not Here

**Lore-native, and a rule rather than a description.**

No livestock. Every noun in this register has something on the other
side of the veil, and a detail that maps onto nothing is decoration.

No explanations, either. A mapping can be perfectly accurate and still
be wrong to set down here, because saying how the trick is done pierces
the veil. That is the same rule arrived at from the opposite side:
livestock fails for having no referent, an explanation fails for showing
one. Everything here has a referent, and none of it says so.
